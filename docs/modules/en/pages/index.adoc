= Seal and publish a series of records
:page-type: how-to
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This guide takes a public body from a series of outbound records to records that meet the `seal-log` profile.
Each step cites the rule it meets.
It is not legal advice, and the page of questions for counsel lists the points that a lawyer must settle.

== Before you seal

Choose the profile of the series, under LOG-002.
A gazette, a register extract or a published decision takes at least `seal-log`.
A series that you keep permanently takes `seal-log-preserve`.
So does a series whose records must stay verifiable past 31 December 2030.
That date holds for the seal algorithms of today.
Write the profile in the inventory of the deployment.

Find the rule of your retention schedule that covers the series, under OBS-102.
Ask your archives whether each yearly shard of the log needs its own rule, under LOG-084.

== Set up the seal

Ask a certificate authority for an organizational seal certificate that names your body, under LOG-014.
Generate its key in a hardware security module, with two people present, under LOG-016 and LOG-062.
Choose ECDSA on P-384 with SHA-384, under LOG-015.

Publish your trust-anchor sets at a stable address, under LOG-017.
List in them the roots of your seal certificate and of your time-stamp authority, under LOG-021.

== Seal each record

Pick the format from the media type of the record, under LOG-011.
A {abbr-pdf-first} file takes PAdES, a {abbr-json-first} file takes JAdES, and an {abbr-xml-first} file takes XAdES.
Seal at level B-LT, with a time stamp of an authority under LOG-020.
Write a seal manifest beside the record, under LOG-019.
The file `examples/seal-manifest.json` shows one.

== Log each record

Draw a random salt of 32 bytes for the record, and keep it with the record, under LOG-031.
Add SHA-256 over the salt, then the sealed bytes, to the log of the current year, under LOG-031 and LOG-035.
Keep the sealed bytes exactly as you logged them, and deliver those bytes.
Never add the record itself, or a hash without salt.

Publish the witness policy of the shard before the first entry, under LOG-040.
It names at least two public witnesses, three if you can, and at least one domestic witness, under LOG-041.
The file `examples/records-log.tlog-policy` shows one.
Ask an archives to mirror the log, under LOG-037.

== Deliver the proof

Wait until the witnesses of the quorum cosign a checkpoint that covers the record, under LOG-046.
Then deliver the proof beside the record, with the salt in its `extra` line, under LOG-050 and LOG-051.
For a record that is not public, send the proof only to those who may receive the record, under LOG-052.
The file `examples/sealed-record.tlog-proof` shows a proof.

== Check the result

Run the example check on your own files, with the same four file names as the folder `examples/`.

[source,text]
----
python3 examples/check-examples.py path/to/your/files
----

A pass shows that the signatures, the quorum, the leaf, the inclusion proof and the manifest hold together.
The check counts only Ed25519 cosignatures toward the quorum.
It does not count ML-DSA-44 cosignatures, which LOG-043 recommends, so a policy of ML-DSA-44 witnesses fails it.
It does not check the seal, which needs a validator under LOG-054.
A conformance tool will follow, under LOG-005.

== Watch the log

Run a monitor that compares each entry of the log with your register, under LOG-060 and LOG-061.
Write the incident plan of LOG-063, and test it once a year.
