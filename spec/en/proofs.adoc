= Proofs and verification
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A citizen, a court or another government receives a sealed record and wants to know three things.
Who issued it, whether anyone changed it, and whether the issuer published it to everyone in the same form.
These rules set the proof that travels with each record, and how any verifier checks it.
They apply to the issuer and to each verifier.

The proof works offline, like a detached signature.
It holds the {term-inclusion-proof} of the record and a checkpoint with its cosignatures.
A verifier with the published policy and trust anchors needs nothing else from the issuer.

== The proof of a record

[[LOG-050]]
*LOG-050.* An issuer MUST deliver a proof with each record of the `seal-log` profiles.
The proof MUST follow the format `c2sp.org/tlog-proof@v1` of link:https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/b2dd9892338af7009733a4575a6da05811ffae6c/tlog-proof.md[C2SP tlog-proof].
Its checkpoint MUST carry the cosignatures that the quorum of LOG-041 needs.
The issuer MUST deliver it beside the record, or in an outer bundle that is not logged.
A proof beside the record is a file with the extension `.tlog-proof`.
The file `examples/sealed-record.tlog-proof` shows a proof.

The proof comes after logging, so it cannot sit inside the logged bytes of LOG-031.

[[LOG-051]]
*LOG-051.* The salt of LOG-031 MUST reach the holder of the record, so that a verifier can rebuild the entry.
The issuer MUST put it in the `extra` line of the proof, or in the outer bundle of LOG-050.
In the `extra` line, it MUST be the raw salt, in canonical base64 under link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc4648[RFC 4648].
A verifier MUST compute the entry as SHA-256 over the salt, then the sealed bytes.
It then computes the leaf hash and checks the inclusion proof against the root of the checkpoint.
The `extra` line has no signature, which does no harm, because a wrong salt fails to rebuild the leaf.

[[LOG-052]]
*LOG-052.* An issuer MUST give a proof that holds the salt only to the parties that may receive the record.
For a record that is not public, it MUST NOT publish such a proof.
It MAY publish the proof of a public record, such as a gazette or a published decision, with its salt.
With the salt, anyone who guesses the content of a record could test the guess against the log.

== Verification

[[LOG-053]]
*LOG-053.* A verifier MUST be able to check a record with published material only.
That material is the sealed record, its proof, the policy, the trust-anchor sets and the time-stamp tokens of the checkpoints.
The check MUST NOT need a call to the issuer.
An issuer MUST offer at least one verifier under an open-source license, which anyone can run on their own device.
A check that depends on the server of the issuer proves only what that server says today.

[[LOG-054]]
*LOG-054.* A verifier MUST make each of these checks and report each result on its own.

1. The seal, under link:https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/319100_319199/31910201/01.04.01_60/en_31910201v010401p.pdf[ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1], through a trust-anchor set of the issuer.
2. The time stamp of the seal, under LOG-020.
3. For the `seal-log` profiles, the log signature of the checkpoint, under the key of the policy.
4. For the same profiles, the cosignatures of the checkpoint and the quorum of the policy, under LOG-045.
5. For the same profiles, the leaf and the inclusion proof, under LOG-051.
6. For records with an {term-evidence-record}, that record, under LOG-070.

A verifier MUST reject base64 that is not canonical, as the C2SP formats ask.
It MUST report the seal result in the form of link:https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/119100_119199/11910202/01.04.01_60/ts_11910202v010401p.pdf[ETSI TS 119 102-2 V1.4.1].
It MUST name the trust-anchor set and the assurance level of LOG-018.
A reader can then see which proof holds and which fails, rather than one yes or no.

[[LOG-055]]
*LOG-055.* An issuer and a verifier MUST NOT present a seal image, a logo or a printed mark as proof.
An issuer MAY show a seal image on a record.
Its verification page MUST then state that only the checks of LOG-054 prove the record.
A copy that someone prints to a new {abbr-pdf-first} file keeps the image and loses the seal.
The link:https://www.govinfo.gov/about/authentication[GovInfo service] of the United States warns its readers about this case.

[[LOG-056]]
*LOG-056.* An issuer SHOULD print a short look-up code and a QR code on each printout of a sealed record.
The code SHOULD lead to an address that serves the sealed original and its proof.
The verification page at that address MUST run the checks of LOG-054 on that material, and show each result.
The code MUST NOT hold personal information.
For a record that is not public, the code MUST hold at least 128 random bits.
Nobody can then guess the code of another record.

A printout cannot carry a seal, so the code leads the reader back to one.

== Optional receipts

[[LOG-057]]
*LOG-057.* A log operator MAY also issue receipts in the form of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9942[RFC 9942], drawn from the same tree.
Such receipts follow the architecture of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9943[RFC 9943].
They MUST NOT replace the proof of LOG-050.
These receipts carry no witness cosignature, and no published text maps a C2SP checkpoint to them yet.
They help tools of the software supply chain read the same log.
