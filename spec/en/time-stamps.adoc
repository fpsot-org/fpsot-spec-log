= Time stamps
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A {term-time-stamp} is a token in which a trusted authority signs a hash and the time it saw it.
It proves that a record existed in that form at that time.
These rules set where the time of a seal and of a checkpoint comes from.
They apply to the issuer, to the log operator and to any verifier.

The research for this specification found no qualified time-stamp authority in Canada or Québec.
The rules therefore take the European policy standard as the bar.
An issuer can meet it with an authority outside Europe.

== Time stamps of seals

[[LOG-020]]
*LOG-020.* An issuer MUST take the time stamp of each seal from a {abbr-tsa-first} that follows link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc3161[RFC 3161].
The authority MUST operate under a policy that meets link:https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/319400_319499/319421/01.03.01_60/en_319421v010301p.pdf[ETSI EN 319 421 V1.3.1].
An equivalent policy that the issuer publishes MAY take its place.
Some issuers want the effect of qualified time stamps in the European Union.
Such an issuer MUST use a {abbr-tsa} that a trusted list of the European Union names as qualified.
The time comes from a party other than the issuer, so the issuer cannot date a record after the fact.

The guidance of the {abbr-tbs-first} asks for a trusted time stamp at its highest level of assurance.
The European rules on qualified time stamps cite the same version of EN 319 421.
They do so in link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1929/oj/eng[Implementing Regulation 2025/1929 of the European Union].

[[LOG-021]]
*LOG-021.* An issuer MUST list each {abbr-tsa} that it uses in a trust-anchor set of LOG-017.
The entry MUST give the policy identifier and the certificates of the authority.
Each request to an authority MUST hash its input with SHA-384 or a stronger hash.
ETSI TS 119 312 V2.1.1 asks for SHA-384 or stronger when proofs must last beyond 20 years.

[[LOG-022]]
*LOG-022.* The `sealedAt` field of the seal manifest MUST hold the time of the time-stamp token.
It MUST NOT hold the time of a local clock.
Each system that seals or logs records MUST synchronize its clock under OBS-033.
A verifier then reads one time, which a party other than the issuer vouched for.

== Time stamps of checkpoints

[[LOG-023]]
*LOG-023.* A log operator MUST take a time stamp over at least one checkpoint each day.
It SHOULD take one over each checkpoint.
The token MUST cover the SHA-384 hash of the checkpoint text followed by the log signature line.
That hash MUST NOT cover the cosignatures.
It MUST come from an authority of LOG-020.
The log operator MUST publish each token beside its checkpoint.

Witnesses add their cosignatures later, so the hash must not depend on them.
An Ed25519 log signature holds no time, as OBS-112 notes.
The token ties every record under that checkpoint to a trusted time, and it starts the evidence record of LOG-075.

[[LOG-024]]
*LOG-024.* A verifier MUST NOT treat the time inside a witness cosignature as a trusted time stamp.
It MAY report the gap between the cosignature time and the time stamp of LOG-023.
Each witness asserts its own time, and no rule binds that clock.
A cosignature time therefore supports the time stamp, and does not replace it.
