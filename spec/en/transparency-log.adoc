= Transparency log
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A {term-transparency-log} is an append-only list of entries in a Merkle tree.
Each entry here commits to one sealed record, through a salted hash.
The log operator signs, at regular times, a {term-checkpoint} that gives the size of the tree and its root hash.
Anyone can then check that a record is in the log, and that the log only grows.

The FPSOT observability specification already sets such a tree for audit records, under OBS-110 to OBS-117.
This page applies those rules to sealed records and changes none of them.
It adds rules where a public log of records needs more than an internal audit log.
Two leaf formats in one ecosystem would double the code of every verifier.

These rules apply to the issuer and to the log operator.

== The tree and the leaves

[[LOG-030]]
*LOG-030.* A log operator MUST meet OBS-110, OBS-113, OBS-115 and OBS-117 for its log.
It applies OBS-116 with its own requirement levels.
In those rules, a sealed record takes the place of an audit record.
The log operator takes the place of the log store.
The witness rule OBS-114 also applies, and LOG-041 makes it stricter.

The tree is therefore the Merkle tree of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc6962[RFC 6962].
The log operator keeps each leaf hash for the life of the tree.

[[LOG-031]]
*LOG-031.* Each entry of the log MUST be the salted hash of OBS-111.
That hash is SHA-256 over a random salt of 32 bytes or more, then the hashed data.
The hashed data MUST be the exact sealed bytes of the record, byte for byte, at the moment of logging.
The issuer MUST keep those bytes and deliver them without change.
These bytes are the PAdES file, the JAdES serialization, the XAdES document or the ASiC-E container.
They take the place of the canonical form that OBS-111 sets for log records.

Each record MUST have its own salt, from a cryptographically secure random generator.
The issuer MUST keep each salt with its sealed record, under OBS-111.
The issuer, and not the log operator, MUST recompute the leaves under OBS-115.
The leaf hash in the tree is the leaf hash of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc6962[RFC 6962] for this entry, under OBS-110.

The log MUST NOT hold the content of a record or an unsalted hash of it.
It MUST NOT hold any other data about the record.
A new serialization would break the seal, so the log commits to the bytes that the seal covers.
OBS-111 keeps its own canonical form for the log stores of the observability specification.

== Checkpoints and keys

[[LOG-032]]
*LOG-032.* A log operator MUST sign checkpoints with an Ed25519 log signature, under OBS-112.
The format is that of link:https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/b2dd9892338af7009733a4575a6da05811ffae6c/tlog-checkpoint.md[C2SP tlog-checkpoint v1.1.0].
The origin line MUST name the issuer and the shard of LOG-035.
It SHOULD have the form `<issuer domain>/<series name>-<year>`, such as `records.example.org/notices-2027`.
For a records log, the issuer domain takes the place of the operator domain of OBS-112.
The policy of LOG-042 names the operator.

A log operator MAY add an ML-DSA-44 cosignature of its own, as OBS-114 allows.
The origin is then the name of that cosigner.

[[LOG-033]]
*LOG-033.* A checkpoint MUST NOT have extension lines.
An ML-DSA-44 cosignature covers only the first three lines of a checkpoint.
Monitors cannot audit extension lines either, and the format advises against them.
A line that one signature covers and another does not would weaken the proof without any reader noticing.

[[LOG-034]]
*LOG-034.* A log operator MUST keep and publish its keys under OBS-113.
The log key MUST differ from every seal key, and MUST sign only the checkpoints of its log.
A log operator SHOULD use a new log key for each new shard.
A leak of one key then touches one shard, and a reader can tell which key signed which year.

== Shards, interface and mirrors

[[LOG-035]]
*LOG-035.* A log operator MUST start a new log, called a shard, at the start of each calendar year.
Years count in {abbr-utc-first}, and an entry belongs to the year in which the log takes it.
It MUST also start a new shard before any change of hash or signature algorithm.
To close a shard, it MUST sign a final checkpoint and collect the quorum of LOG-041 on it.
It MUST time-stamp that checkpoint under LOG-023, then accept no new entry in that shard.
A closed shard MUST stay readable for its retention period, under LOG-084.

link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9162[RFC 9162] forbids a change of algorithm in a live log, and asks that a retired log stay readable.
Yearly shards also match the yearly key rotation of OBS-113, and give each shard a clear retention rule.

[[LOG-036]]
*LOG-036.* A log operator MUST serve each shard through the read interface of link:https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/b2dd9892338af7009733a4575a6da05811ffae6c/tlog-tiles.md[C2SP tlog-tiles v1.0.0].
It MUST NOT prune a shard, so its minimum index stays zero.
Anyone can then fetch every entry and every proof without an account, and the same tools read every FPSOT log.

[[LOG-037]]
*LOG-037.* An issuer MUST arrange that at least one independent mirror keeps a full copy of each shard.
The copy includes closed shards.
An organization other than the issuer MUST run it.
Before it stores new entries, the mirror MUST check that the new checkpoint extends the last one it stored.
It MUST serve its copy through the interface of LOG-036.
A mirror SHOULD follow link:https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/b2dd9892338af7009733a4575a6da05811ffae6c/tlog-mirror.md[C2SP tlog-mirror v0.1.0], and cosign the checkpoints that it stores.

A national or provincial archives is a natural mirror.
The log then outlives its operator, and an operator that closes cannot take its history away.

The mirror protocol has only reached version 0.1.0.
A later version of this specification plans to make it a MUST once the protocol reaches version 1.

[[LOG-038]]
*LOG-038.* A log MUST hold entries for the records of one issuer only.
Its write interface MUST accept entries only from the sealing service of that issuer, after authentication.
The monitor of LOG-061 can then treat any entry that the issuer did not expect as an incident.
