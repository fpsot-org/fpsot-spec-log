= Algorithm agility and post-quantum
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A large quantum computer could forge the classical signatures that seals use today.
Governments have set dates for the change.
In Canada, link:https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/cryptographic-algorithms-unclassified-protected-protected-b-information-itsp40111[ITSP.40.111] phases out RSA, ECDSA and EdDSA signatures by the end of 2035.
Only a pair with a post-quantum scheme stays in use after that date.

The federal roadmap sets the end of 2031 for systems of high priority.
The European roadmap counts long-term retention among the cases of high risk, with a deadline at the end of 2030.
ETSI TS 119 312 V2.1.1 asks for a hybrid or post-quantum seal when its validity goes past 2030.

No validator can check a hybrid seal in the AdES formats today.
ETSI has not yet said how to carry ML-DSA in JAdES or XAdES.
These rules therefore ask for agility now, and the evidence records of LOG-070 bridge the gap.
They apply to the issuer, to the log operator, to witnesses and to verifiers.

== Agility

[[LOG-090]]
*LOG-090.* Each seal, each time stamp, each signature or cosignature and each evidence record MUST name its algorithm.
It MUST do so in the field that its format defines.
An implementation MUST take its algorithms from configuration, not from its code.
A change of algorithm MUST NOT need a change of format or of the rules of this specification.
An issuer can then change algorithms by configuration, on the date that its plan sets.

[[LOG-091]]
*LOG-091.* A verifier MUST accept a record that carries more than one seal.
It MUST check every seal whose algorithm it supports.
The `hybridWith` field of the seal manifest names the other seal of a hybrid pair, under LOG-019.
The verifier MUST report such a pair as passed only if both seals pass.
If it cannot check one of the two, it MUST report the pair as not verified.
ETSI TS 119 312 V2.1.1 asks for both signatures of a hybrid to verify.

[[LOG-092]]
*LOG-092.* An issuer MUST keep an inventory of the keys and algorithms of its seals, time stamps, log and witnesses.
The inventory MUST give the end date of each.
It MUST review the inventory at least once a year, against ETSI TS 119 312 and ITSP.40.111.
It MUST plan its move to post-quantum algorithms on the milestones of link:https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/roadmap-migration-post-quantum-cryptography-government-canada-itsm40001[ITSM.40.001].
An issuer outside Canada MAY use the roadmap of its own {term-jurisdiction} instead.
A body that knows where each key sits can change it before the deadline, and not after an incident.

[[LOG-093]]
*LOG-093.* A verifier MUST accept checkpoint cosignatures of the two kinds of link:https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/b2dd9892338af7009733a4575a6da05811ffae6c/tlog-checkpoint.md[C2SP tlog-checkpoint v1.1.0].
These are ML-DSA-44 cosignatures, of type `0x06`, and Ed25519 cosignatures, of type `0x04`.
LOG-043 recommends ML-DSA-44 for witnesses, so a verifier that reads only Ed25519 would miss the quorum.

== Planned change

This section is informative.
A later version of this specification plans to require hybrid seals.
Each would pair ECDSA on P-384 with ML-DSA-87 of link:https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final[FIPS 204], as ETSI TS 119 312 recommends for lasting seals.
Two events will trigger the change.
ETSI must publish how to carry ML-DSA in the formats of LOG-010, and an open-source validator must support it.
The change will apply at the latest to records sealed after 2030.

Until then, the seal keys of LOG-015 stay valid, and the issuer keeps them when it adds the new key.

The final link:https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final[NIST IR 8547] of October 2026 also sets a path away from classical signatures.
