= Retention, redaction and transfer
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A sealed record follows the same retention schedule as any other record of its issuer.
This specification adds no duty to keep a record longer.
The log, though, must keep every leaf, or the proofs of the other records would break.
The salted hash of LOG-031 resolves this tension.
When the issuer destroys a record and its salt, the leaf that remains reveals nothing.
Every other proof still holds.

These rules reuse the retention rules of the observability specification, and change none of them.
They apply to the issuer, to the log operator and to the preservation service.

== Retention and destruction

[[LOG-080]]
*LOG-080.* An issuer MUST meet OBS-100, OBS-102, OBS-103 and OBS-104 for its sealed records.
It applies OBS-116 with its own requirement levels.
These rules also cover the salts, the seal manifests and the entries of the register of LOG-060.
In those rules, a sealed record takes the place of a log record.

Each retention rule then names the rule of the schedule that it applies.
A {term-legal-hold} stops destruction, and each destruction leaves an audit event that names the latest checkpoint.
The retention profile of OBS-101 does not apply.
Only the schedule of the issuer sets how long a sealed record stays.

[[LOG-081]]
*LOG-081.* To destroy a record, an issuer MUST destroy the sealed record and its salt together, in every copy.
It MUST NOT remove or change the log entry of the record.
The destruction event of OBS-104 MUST give the shard and the index of the entry.
Without the salt, nobody can test a guess against the leaf.
The leaf that remains then says nothing about the record.
The tree keeps its leaf, and the proofs of every other record stay valid.

[[LOG-082]]
*LOG-082.* An issuer MUST NOT change a sealed record.
To correct a record, it MUST seal and log a new record.
The `replaces` field of the new manifest MUST name the old record.
To withdraw a record, it MUST create the event `org.fpsot.log.record.withdrawn`, and then keep or destroy the record under its schedule.
To redact a record, it MUST withdraw it and seal a redacted copy, never remove a leaf.
Readers then see the whole history, and the log stays append-only.

== Transfer

[[LOG-083]]
*LOG-083.* An issuer MUST keep a transfer record when it moves a sealed record to a new format.
It MUST do the same when it seals a record again, and the transfer record follows OBS-105.
It MUST give the `id` and the log entry of the old record.
It MUST also give the `id` and the seal of the new record, the method and the date.
The new record MUST get its own log entry, for the `seal-log` profiles.
The issuer MUST keep the old sealed record for as long as its retention rule asks, under OBS-102.

A change of format breaks a seal.
The transfer record then carries the chain of proof from the old record to the new one.

A Québec act asks to document the transfer of a document to another medium.
That act sets the legal framework for information technology.
A link:https://www.barreaudemontreal.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017-guideti-documenttechnologique_fev2017.pdf[secondary source] reads its article 17 as asking for three facts, _to be verified_.
They are the original format, the method and the guarantees of integrity.

== Log shards

[[LOG-084]]
*LOG-084.* An issuer MUST treat each closed shard as a records series, under OBS-102.
The series MUST have its own rule in the retention schedule.
The issuer MUST keep permanently each shard with an entry of a permanent record.
It MUST also keep that shard's mirror copy and its evidence records.
It MAY destroy a whole shard once every record in it has reached its end, and no legal hold applies.

Yearly shards make this tractable, since each shard has a clear content and a clear end.
Whether a log shard is a document that a retention schedule must cover is a question for counsel.

[[LOG-085]]
*LOG-085.* A log, its checkpoints, its policies and the proofs that an issuer publishes MUST NOT hold personal information.
The salted hash of LOG-031 MUST be the only data about a record in the log.
An issuer MUST NOT log an unsalted hash of a record or of an identifier.
A social insurance number is such an identifier.
The {term-privacy-impact-assessment} of the system SHOULD cover the log.

Anyone can recover a short value from its unsalted hash, by trying every candidate.
Whether a salted leaf of a destroyed record is still personal information is a question for counsel.
