= Questions for counsel
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
It lists the legal questions that this specification raises but cannot answer.
A lawyer must answer each one before {abbr-fpsot-first} or an issuer relies on it.
The research read the Québec statutes through secondary sources, because their official site refuses automated readers.
An article number marked _to be verified_ comes from such a source, not from the law itself.

== Seals and evidence

1. *Seal of a body.* A Québec act on information technology ties the legal value of a document to its integrity.
A signature binds a document while the link between them holds.
Does a record with an organizational seal and a witnessed log proof meet both conditions?
The articles at stake are 5 to 7, 38 and 39, _to be verified_.
Is an organizational seal a signature under article 2827 of the Civil Code of Québec, _to be verified_?
Or is it only a means to link a body to a document?

2. *Foreign seals.* The European Union gives some seals and signatures the status of qualified.
Does such a seal get any presumption before a Québec or federal court?
Or does it carry only the weight of ordinary evidence?
The rules at stake are section 31.1 of the link:https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-5/page-3.html[Canada Evidence Act].
They also include article 38 of the Québec act, _to be verified_.
A secondary source reports a memorandum on trust services between Canada and the European Union in December 2025.
Would that memorandum change the answer?

3. *Federal seals and originals.* Two sections of a federal act cover seals and originals.
They are sections 39 and 42 of the link:https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/page-5.html[Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act].
A federal institution may want to rely on them for a seal of this specification.
Can it do so only when Schedule 2 or 3 lists the governing statute?
Must the certificate authority be one that the President of the Treasury Board recognizes under section 4 of SOR/2005-30?
The guidance of the {abbr-tbs-first} says that this recognition process is under review.

4. *Québec government certificates.* Is the 2014 directive on the certification services of the Québec government still in force?
Must a Québec public body use its certificates for outbound seals, or may it use another certificate authority?

5. *Weight of the specification.* A court may hear evidence of a practice for storing records.
Section 31.5 of the Canada Evidence Act allows it.
Could a court give weight to this specification on that basis?
Could it do so under articles 15 and 68 of the Québec act, _to be verified_?
Would certification against CAN/CGSB-72.34-2024 strengthen that weight?
Could {abbr-fpsot} count as a recognized body under article 68, _to be verified_?

6. *Time stamps from abroad.* Some time-stamp authorities sit outside Canada and the European Union.
Can such a {abbr-tsa-first} time-stamp Québec records that a court will rely on?
Does a qualified time stamp of the European Union add any weight in Canada?

== Records, privacy and retention

1. *Leaves of destroyed records.* After a lawful destruction under a retention schedule, the log keeps a salted hash.
The salt is gone.
Is that leaf still personal information under the Québec access act or the federal Privacy Act?

2. *The log as a document.* Is a log shard a document that a retention schedule must cover?
The schedule would be that of a Québec body, or a federal disposition authority.
May a body keep a shard permanently when it holds the leaves of records that the body must destroy?

3. *Privacy impact assessment.* Does a public log of salted hashes of records that hold personal information need a {term-privacy-impact-assessment}?
Secondary sources point to article 63.5 of the Québec access act, _to be verified_.

4. *Transfers.* Article 17 of the Québec act asks to document a transfer, _to be verified_.
Section 37 of the federal act sets conditions on retention.
Does a transfer record under LOG-083 meet both?
Must the original sealed file then stay, and for how long?

== Witnesses, mirrors and software

1. *Liability of a witness.* A public body or {abbr-fpsot} may cosign the log of another government.
What liability follows from a wrong or a missing cosignature?
Should the terms of a witness disclaim any reliance?

2. *Witnesses abroad.* Some witnesses and mirrors run outside Canada, and they see only checkpoints and hashes.
Does that raise a question of data residency, or of transfer of personal information outside Québec?

3. *License of the seal library.* The seal library of the page xref:reference-stack.adoc[Reference stack] has a weak copyleft license.
It is under the GNU Lesser General Public License 2.1.
May {abbr-fpsot} link it into a native executable that ships with software under the Apache License 2.0?
Which materials must it then supply?
A user must be able to link the program again with another version of the library.
